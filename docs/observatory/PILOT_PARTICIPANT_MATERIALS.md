## Participant information draft

### What is the study?

We are testing GOV.UK Delegate, an independent prototype that records what an assistant has permission to do and lets a person remove that permission. We want to see how people understand and complete its steps. The software uses synthetic accounts and tax records. You are testing the prototype, not your ability to use a computer.

[Named study owner and organisation] is responsible for this study. Contact [research contact] with questions or [approved privacy/complaints contact] about the information we keep. This study is a small feasibility test. It will not prove that Delegate saves money or that a computer can reliably detect AI use.

### What will I do?

In [approved duration and format], you will use a simulated account and made-up instructions to [approved tasks: inspect status, give limited permissions, inspect an outcome and remove access]. Some tasks may involve [approved AI tool and exact assistance arrangement]. You make the permission choices yourself. The researcher will observe the steps and may ask you to explain who can act, which actions are excluded and what happens when access is removed.

Use only the supplied fictional details. Do not enter real tax, bank, benefit or identity information, private messages, a personal password or a personal account. [Approved arrangements for study-provided tools/accounts.] You may pause, skip a task, decline an optional tool or stop. Tell us what support or alternative format would help; using accessibility tools is not treated as suspicious.

### What information will we record?

We plan to record a study code, the steps and their timing, requests for help, coded answers to the permission questions, and what the prototype actually did. The prototype records permission checks and service actions using research identifiers. A reviewer checks the evidence separately from the prototype's observations.

[Approved recording/observation method and exact trace fields.] We will not collect real tax details, passwords, private prompt text, model reasoning, raw IP addresses or unrelated browsing/device information. Contact and agreement records will be kept separately from the coded session evidence in [approved restricted storage]. A study code makes records pseudonymous; it does not make all records anonymous.

[Choose and disclose the actual arrangement before release. For a sensor-disabled pilot, state that optional device/vendor tracking is off. You do not have to agree to an optional AI tool or optional device tracking to ask questions about the study. [Approved alternative and how declining changes the available tasks.] Nothing the research tool predicts changes your permissions or the service's decision.

### Who can see it, and how long is it kept?

[Named roles with access to contact/consent records, coded observations and technical traces; approved storage/export arrangements.] Reports will contain [approved deidentified summaries] and will not include your name, contact details, consent document or private conversation.

[Approved retention dates for each record type, including copies/backups and any external tool/vendor records.] The technical protocol proposes a maximum 30-day raw-event period; the owner must choose and approve the actual arrangements before this information is used. You can stop the session at any time by telling the researcher. To ask for removal of your study records, contact [approved contact and method] with [approved private study-code arrangement].
