## About this project

### Why we are exploring Delegate

Software agents will increasingly be able to fill in forms, submit evidence, report changes and carry out other government transactions for people.

It is difficult to reliably determine whether a piece of text was written by AI after it has been submitted.

We are testing a different approach: **make it easier for legitimate agents to identify themselves and prove that they have permission to act.**

If legitimate agents have a safe, useful route into government services, government can spend less effort guessing which ordinary-looking transactions are automated and more effort controlling activity that stays outside the trusted route.

### The idea

A person signs in with GOV.UK One Login.

They can then give a delegate limited authority to act for them.

For example, they might allow an agent to:

- check a claim status;
- submit evidence;
- report a change.

But not allow it to:

- change payment details;
- withdraw a claim.

The person can remove the authority at any time.

### Trusted lane

Authenticated people and authorised agents can use a trusted lane.

That could mean better machine interfaces, clearer errors and appropriate throughput.

Traffic whose identity or authority cannot be established can be subject to more checks and tighter limits.

**The aim is to make legitimate automation easier than illegitimate automation.**

### Accessibility and inclusion

A trusted route only works if legitimate people can enter it.

Some people will need help proving identity, managing delegates or completing an authorisation journey. The service should identify those barriers and provide supported alternatives rather than treating difficulty using digital services as suspicious behaviour.

### What this prototype does not claim

This prototype does not detect whether text was generated by AI.

It does not stop every malicious bot.

It does not connect to real DWP claimant information.

It tests whether explicit, machine-checkable delegation gives government a better foundation for managing agent activity.

## Existing government patterns

### HMRC agent authorisation

HMRC already lets registered tax agents request permission to act for clients. The client independently approves the request, and HMRC records a relationship that can later be checked or removed.

This is the main product pattern we are copying.

### GOV.UK One Login

One Login already provides a common way for services to authenticate users and, where needed, prove identity.

Delegate explores the next relationship: **who is allowed to act for that authenticated person?**

### Government delegated-authority guidance

Existing government guidance says evidence of authority needs to identify the subject and representative, describe the permissions granted and record when they were given. It can also include conditions such as time or value limits.

### DWP representation and explicit consent

DWP already allows claimants to give representatives access to information about their claim, and Universal Credit explicit consent can be limited and withdrawn.

The prototype explores how that concept might become a reusable digital transaction capability rather than a service-specific process.

### Use an LPA

The Office of the Public Guardian already uses One Login in its online LPA service and provides time-limited access codes that organisations can use to verify authority.

### What this tells us
